## A. Lexical family — what's established

- **Yakura et al. 2024/2025, "Empirical evidence of LLM's influence on human spoken communication"** ([arXiv:2409.01754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01754); Max Planck Institute). ~740k hours of podcast/YouTube speech; ChatGPT-preferred words (delve, meticulous, boast, showcase, intricacies) rose abruptly post-release; synthetic-control analysis. Population-level; explicitly cannot tie change to individual exposure. Our fingerprint-word candidates and the "spontaneous speech" strategy come from here.
- **Anderson, Galpin et al., AIES 2025 (FSU), "Model Misalignment and Language Change"** ([press](https://news.fsu.edu/news/education-society/2025/08/26/on-screen-and-now-irl-fsu-researchers-find-evidence-suggesting-chatgpt-influences-how-we-speak/)). 22.1M words, 1,326 tech/science podcast episodes, pre vs. post; AI-associated words rose while synonyms did not (underscore ↑, accentuate flat) — the synonym-control idea our placebo battery generalizes. Population-level, tech/science genre only.
- **USC 2026, "How LLMs Distort Our Written Language"** ([arXiv:2603.18161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.18161); [NBC coverage](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-changing-style-substance-human-writing-study-finds-rcna263789)). Writing-style diversity collapsed post-ChatGPT across news/science/social media, including among non-users (per authors' interpretation) — population-level homogenization.
- **Kobak et al.** and successors: LLM-word inflation in academic abstracts (PubMed). Written, assisted-authorship-confounded; useful only for fingerprint candidates.
- **"Why Does ChatGPT 'Delve' So Much"** ([arXiv:2412.11385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.11385)): sources of lexical overrepresentation — useful for fingerprint construction rationale.

**Gap our lexical family targets:** no published study measures *individual-level dose-response* — same speakers over time, classified by personal LLM exposure, fingerprint vs. placebo. Confirmed absent in searches to date.

## B. Mechanism — lab evidence that absorption can happen

- **"Cognitive Spillover in Human-AI Teams"** ([ACM TOCHI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3805039); arXiv:2407.17489). Two randomized experiments: AI language leaves a linguistic/conceptual fingerprint that persists into subsequent human-human interaction after the AI is removed (empathic- vs. formal-prompted assistant conditions). Short-term, lab. Establishes mechanism plausibility for both our families.
- Linguistic alignment/entrainment literature (humans align lexically/syntactically with dialogue systems as with humans; e.g., [GPT-3.5/4 lexical alignment](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-54975-5_6)): mature background for the absorption mechanism.

## C. Relational family — what exists (scanned 2026-07-28, after v0.3)

- **"Why sycophantic LLMs may imperil interactive norms between humans"** (Communications Psychology, 2026; [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00486-9)). **The closest work to our relational question — and it is a theory/perspective piece, not an empirical corpus study.** Proposes **"norm leakage"**: cross-context carryover of communicative habits from human-AI exchange into human-human interaction. Notes users address LLMs with more direct, low-hedging language, and cites short-term experimental spillovers (harsher evaluations, reduced cooperation). This gives our relational family its theoretical frame and a citation anchor; it also confirms no one has tested norm leakage observationally at the individual level.
- **Sycophancy-consequence experiments** ([Scientific American coverage](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ai-chatbots-are-sucking-up-to-you-with-consequences-for-your-relationships/); Northeastern/CHI 2026 work on [sycophancy and trust](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791079)): LLMs affirm users ~49% more than humans do; sycophantic advice makes people less likely to apologize and more convinced they're right. Short-term, experimental, advice-taking outcomes — not conversational-style outcomes, not longitudinal.
- **Politeness-strategy comparisons** ([EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.820.pdf)): human vs. LLM politeness strategies characterized — useful for the R2–R5 construct definitions, not a change-over-time study.

**Gap our relational family targets:** no observational, longitudinal, individual-level study of whether human-to-human disagreement style (concessive prefacing, hedging, both-sidesing, directness) has shifted with LLM adoption. The Nature CP piece *predicts* effects in both directions (absorption of LLM softness vs. habituation to command-style bluntness) — which strengthens our design: R2/R3/R4 (softening) and R5 (bluntness) can adjudicate between the two predicted directions, a point worth making explicit in the write-up.
