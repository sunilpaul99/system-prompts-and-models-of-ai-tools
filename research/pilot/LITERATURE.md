# Literature Review — Prioritized Reading List

Compiled 2026-07-30 for the PI. Tiered by priority; within tiers, ordered by
relevance to the pilot's two families (lexical H1-L, relational H1-R).

## Tier 1 — Direct hits: read before freeze (affects our design)

1. **Yakura et al., "Empirical evidence of LLM's influence on human spoken
   communication"** (arXiv:2409.01754, now at v4 and peer-reviewed).
   THE anchor paper. The current version goes far beyond the 2024 preprint:
   synthetic-control analysis of **737k hours / 824k podcast episodes**
   causally linking the lexical shift to ChatGPT's release, PLUS a
   preregistered experiment (N=496) showing a brief chatbot interaction
   entrains vocabulary that persists past a distractor task (58% vs 23%
   uptake). **Design implication for us:** population-level podcast evidence
   and lab-scale individual entrainment now both exist — our niche is the
   missing middle: *naturalistic within-person change conditioned on
   documented real-world adoption*, plus the relational family, which nobody
   has touched. Read fully, including methods appendix.
2. **Anderson, Galpin & Juzek, "Model Misalignment and Language Change:
   Traces of AI-Associated Language in Unscripted Spoken English"**
   (AIES 2025; arXiv:2508.00238). First peer-reviewed evidence in unscripted
   spoken English — 22.1M words of conversational sci/tech podcasts, moderate
   but significant post-2022 increase. Closest methodological cousin to our
   lexical family; note their word list and controls for our Appendix A gates.
3. **Cheng et al. (Science, 2026?), "Sycophantic AI decreases prosocial
   intentions and promotes dependence"** — 11 models affirm users' actions
   ~49% more than humans; preregistered experiments (N=2,405) where a single
   sycophantic interaction reduced willingness to repair conflicts and
   increased conviction of being right — while being preferred and trusted.
   **This is the causal-mechanism backbone for H1-R** (disagreement style as
   the outcome medium). Cite in the protocol's §5.2 rationale.
4. **Kobak et al., "Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical
   publications through excess vocabulary"** (Science Advances; arXiv:2406.07016).
   The excess-vocabulary method on 15M PubMed abstracts; source of several of
   our fingerprint candidates. Key nuance for us: **"delve" spiked then
   DECLINED once it became a recognized AI tell** — fingerprint words can be
   consciously suppressed; our within-person design should expect
   non-monotone trajectories (worth a note in §7 interpretation).

## Tier 2 — Theory underneath (the "why would this happen at all" shelf)

5. **Pickering & Garrod, "Toward a mechanistic psychology of dialogue"**
   (BBS 2004) — interactive alignment: interlocutors automatically converge
   at every level (lexical, syntactic, phonetic). The core mechanism that
   makes LLM→human absorption plausible.
6. **Giles et al., Communication Accommodation Theory** (any modern review) —
   the social-motivation layer on top of automatic alignment: we converge
   toward interlocutors we affiliate with. Predicts moderation by attitude
   toward AI — a variable our exposure dossiers incidentally capture.
7. **Brennan & Clark, "Conceptual pacts and lexical choice in conversation"**
   (JEP:LMC 1996) — lexical entrainment as partner-specific convention
   formation; the classic the N=496 experiment descends from.
8. **Pomerantz, "Agreeing and disagreeing with assessments"** (1984) — the
   conversation-analysis foundation for R2: disagreement is a "dispreferred"
   turn normally marked by delay, hedges, and agreement-prefaces. Our
   concessive-preface measure is literally Pomerantz's preface, so H1-R asks
   whether LLM exposure amplifies a known universal — good framing, and it
   sharpens the rubric definitions.
9. **Brown & Levinson, Politeness theory** (1987, selectively) — hedging and
   face-work vocabulary for R3/R4; skim the hedging taxonomy only.
10. **Cohn & Zellou's voice-AI alignment program** (Interspeech 2020;
    Frontiers 2020/2021; Speech Communication 2021) — humans phonetically
    align toward voice-AI, less than toward humans, modulated by
    expressiveness/role. Evidence that machine interlocutors already reshape
    speech production at fine grain. One representative paper suffices
    ("Social and Functional Pressures in Vocal Alignment").

## Tier 3 — Methods and adjacent (skim / reference as needed)

11. **"Will AI shape the way we speak?"** (arXiv:2504.10650) — synthesis of
    the sociolinguistic influence of synthetic voices; good related-work map.
12. **PNAS, "The diffusion of large language models in published academic
    articles"** — adoption-curve shape (lag, surge, plateau); useful prior
    for our adoption-interval modeling and washout choice.
13. **Juzek & colleagues, "Exploring the Structure of AI-Induced Language
    Change in Scientific English"** (FLAIRS/arXiv:2506.21817) — are spiking
    words REPLACING synonyms or adding meaning? Directly relevant to
    placebo-word matching (a fingerprint word's rise may show as a matched
    synonym's fall — argues for checking synonym displacement in NC1).
14. **"Sycophantic AI makes human interaction feel more effortful and less
    satisfying over time"** (arXiv 2026) — complements #3; longitudinal
    exposure angle.
15. **Persistent lexical entrainment in HRI** (HRI 2017) — robots entrain
    vocabulary that outlasts the interaction; precedent for durability.
16. **"How individuals change language"** (PMC 2021) — individual-level
    mechanisms of language change pre-AI; the historical-linguistics frame
    our estimand lives in.

## Gap analysis (why our pilot still matters after Tier 1)

- Population-level spoken-language shift: ESTABLISHED (Yakura; Anderson).
- Short-horizon individual entrainment in the lab: ESTABLISHED (Yakura exp).
- **Missing: within-person change over years in naturalistic speech,
  conditioned on documented adoption vs non-adoption** — our H1-L. Nobody
  has the exposure-classified panel.
- **Missing entirely: relational/interactional style (disagreement conduct)
  as the outcome** — our H1-R. The Science sycophancy paper supplies the
  mechanism and makes the question urgent; no one has measured it in the
  wild. This is the pilot's clearest claim to novelty.
